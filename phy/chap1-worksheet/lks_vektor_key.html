Force & Electric Field Vector Analysis

3. Skill Test Solutions

Problem 1 (Collinear): Three collinear charges. Charge A (\(+2 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the left, charge B (\(-1 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is in the middle, and charge C (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the right. The distance from A to B is \(3 \, \text{cm}\), and from B to C is \(3 \, \text{cm}\). Determine the magnitude and direction of the total Coulomb force experienced by charge B!

Step 1: Calculate the magnitude of \(F_{BA}\) and \(F_{BC}\)

\[ F_{BA} = k \frac{|q_A \cdot q_B|}{r_{AB}^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{(2 \times 10^{-6})(1 \times 10^{-6})}{(3 \times 10^{-2})^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{2 \times 10^{-12}}{9 \times 10^{-4}} = 20 \, \text{N} \] Direction of \(F_{BA}\): Left (B is attracted to A). \[ F_{BC} = k \frac{|q_B \cdot q_C|}{r_{BC}^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{(1 \times 10^{-6})(4 \times 10^{-6})}{(3 \times 10^{-2})^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{4 \times 10^{-12}}{9 \times 10^{-4}} = 40 \, \text{N} \] Direction of \(F_{BC}\): Right (B is attracted to C).

Step 2: Calculate the Resultant Force (\(\Sigma F_B\))

Assuming Right is positive and Left is negative:

\[ \Sigma F_B = F_{BC} - F_{BA} = 40 \, \text{N} - 20 \, \text{N} = 20 \, \text{N} \]

Final Answer: The total force is \(20 \, \text{N}\) to the Right (towards C).

Problem 2 (Perpendicular): Point P is located \(3 \, \text{cm}\) exactly above charge Q1 (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) and \(4 \, \text{cm}\) to the left of charge Q2 (\(-3 \, \mu\text{C}\)). If the constant \( k = 9 \times 10^9 \), calculate the total electric field strength at point P!

Step 1: Calculate Electric Field from Q1 (\(E_1\))

\[ E_1 = k \frac{|Q_1|}{r_1^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{4 \times 10^{-6}}{(3 \times 10^{-2})^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{4 \times 10^{-6}}{9 \times 10^{-4}} = 4 \times 10^7 \, \text{N/C} \] Direction: Upward (away from positive charge Q1).

Step 2: Calculate Electric Field from Q2 (\(E_2\))

\[ E_2 = k \frac{|Q_2|}{r_2^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{3 \times 10^{-6}}{(4 \times 10^{-2})^2} = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{3 \times 10^{-6}}{16 \times 10^{-4}} = \frac{27}{16} \times 10^7 \, \text{N/C} = 1.6875 \times 10^7 \, \text{N/C} \] Direction: Rightward (towards negative charge Q2).

Step 3: Calculate the Resultant Electric Field (\(E_p\))

Since \(E_1\) is pointing Up and \(E_2\) is pointing Right, they are perpendicular (\(90^\circ\)):

\[ E_p = \sqrt{E_1^2 + E_2^2} = \sqrt{(4 \times 10^7)^2 + (1.6875 \times 10^7)^2} \approx 4.34 \times 10^7 \, \text{N/C} \]

Final Answer: The total electric field is approximately \(4.34 \times 10^7 \, \text{N/C}\).

Problem 3 (Resultant Challenge): Two attractive electrical forces act on a charge. The first force is \( 6 \, \text{N} \) pointing North, and the second force is \( 8 \, \text{N} \) pointing East. What is the magnitude of the resultant force experienced by the charge?

Step 1: Identify the angle

North and East are perpendicular to each other, so the angle is \(90^\circ\). We can use the Pythagorean theorem directly.

Step 2: Calculate the Resultant (\(R\))

\[ R = \sqrt{F_{\text{North}}^2 + F_{\text{East}}^2} \] \[ R = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} \] \[ R = \sqrt{36 + 64} \] \[ R = \sqrt{100} = 10 \, \text{N} \]

Final Answer: The resultant force magnitude is \(10 \, \text{N}\) (Following the 3-4-5 triangle ratio rule: \(2(3)-2(4)-2(5) \rightarrow 6-8-10\)).
