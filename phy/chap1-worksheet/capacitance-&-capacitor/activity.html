Warm-Up — Spark Your Thinking

10 minutes

No calculators yet. Just think, sketch, and predict.

  • 1.In your own words, explain what a capacitor is and why it can "store" charge even though the dielectric between its plates doesn't conduct electricity.
  • 2.Sketch a simple parallel-plate capacitor. Label the two plates, the dielectric, and the sign of the charge on each plate.
  • 3.Predict: if you push the plates of a capacitor closer together, what do you expect happens to its capacitance? Write your prediction and your reasoning before you check the formula in Stage 1.

Stage 1 — Define & Derive

15 minutes
Q
C
V

Math Hint: The Magic Triangle

Cover the letter you want to find. If the remaining letters are top and bottom, you divide. If they are side-by-side, you multiply.
\( Q = C \times V \quad|\quad C = \frac{Q}{V} \quad|\quad V = \frac{Q}{C} \)

Practice using \( C = \frac{Q}{V} \) and the parallel-plate formula.

  • 1.A capacitor stores a charge of \( 6 \times 10^{-4} \text{ C} \) when connected across a 12 V battery. Calculate its capacitance. Given: \( Q = 6 \times 10^{-4} \text{ C} \), \( V = 12 \text{ V} \rightarrow \) Find \( C \)
  • 2.A 470 µF capacitor is connected to a 9 V supply. How much charge does it store? Given: \( V = 9 \text{ V} \), \( C = 470 \, \mu\text{F} \)
    Reminder: \( 470 \, \mu\text{F} = 470 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F} \) \( \rightarrow \) Find \( Q \)
  • 3.A parallel-plate capacitor has plates of area \( 0.02 \text{ m}^2 \), separated by 0.5 mm of air (\( \varepsilon_r = 1 \)). Calculate its capacitance. (\( \varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m} \)) Given: \( A = 0.02 \text{ m}^2 \), \( d = 0.5 \text{ mm} \)
    Reminder: Convert mm to m (\( 0.5 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m} \)) \( \rightarrow \) Find \( C \) using \( C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d} \)
  • 4.The same plates from question 3 are now separated by a dielectric with \( \varepsilon_r = 4 \) instead of air, at the same distance. Recalculate the capacitance, and explain in one sentence why it changed.
  • 5.Revisit your prediction from Warm-Up question 3. Was it correct? Use the formula to justify your answer.

Stage 2 — Series & Parallel Circuits

20 minutes

Combine capacitors and track how charge and voltage distribute.

  • 1.For the series circuit shown (\( C_1 = 6 \, \mu\text{F} \), \( C_2 = 3 \, \mu\text{F} \), supply \( V = 18 \text{ V} \)), calculate the total (equivalent) capacitance. Shortcut Hint: For two series capacitors, use \( C_{\text{total}} = \frac{C_1 \times C_2}{C_1 + C_2} \)
  • 2.Using your answer above, calculate the total charge supplied by the battery, and state the charge stored on \( C_1 \) and on \( C_2 \). Hint: Find \( Q_{\text{total}} \) first (\( Q = C_{\text{total}} \times V \)). Remember, in series, \( Q_1 = Q_2 = Q_{\text{total}} \).
  • 3.Find the voltage across \( C_1 \) and across \( C_2 \) individually, and check that they add up to 18 V.
  • 4.For the parallel circuit shown (\( C_3 = 4 \, \mu\text{F} \), \( C_4 = 2 \, \mu\text{F} \), supply \( V = 10 \text{ V} \)), calculate the total capacitance and the charge stored on each capacitor. Hint: In parallel, voltages are equal (\( V_3 = V_4 = 10 \text{ V} \)). Find \( Q \) for each using \( Q = C \times V \)
  • 5.In two sentences, explain why series combinations always give a smaller total capacitance while parallel combinations always give a larger one.

U

Stage 3 — Energy Storage Calculations

15 minutes

Use \( U = \frac{1}{2}QV = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C} \) to find stored energy.

  • 1.A 220 µF capacitor is charged to 25 V. Calculate the energy stored. Given: \( C = 220 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F} \), \( V = 25 \text{ V} \rightarrow \) Use \( U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 \)
  • 2.A capacitor stores \( 4 \times 10^{-3} \text{ C} \) of charge at a potential difference of 40 V. Calculate the energy stored, using a different formula from question 1. Given: \( Q = 4 \times 10^{-3} \text{ C} \), \( V = 40 \text{ V} \rightarrow \) Use \( U = \frac{1}{2}QV \)
  • 3.Using your results from Stage 2 question 1 (the series combination), calculate the total energy stored in that circuit.
  • 4.Using your results from Stage 2 question 4 (the parallel combination), calculate the total energy stored in that circuit.
  • 5.Between the series and parallel circuits in Stage 2, which one stores more total energy? Suggest a reason why, based on their equivalent capacitance.

Stage 4 — Real-World Application

10 minutes

Reasoning questions — write full-sentence explanations, not just one-word answers.

  • 1.A camera flash uses a capacitor rather than powering the flash bulb directly from the battery. Explain why, using the idea of charge and discharge rate.
  • 2.A defibrillator needs to deliver a large amount of energy to a patient in a fraction of a second. Explain why a capacitor is better suited to this job than a battery alone.
  • 3.Engineers designing a phone touchscreen's capacitor-based sensors need to choose a dielectric material carefully. Suggest one property that dielectric should have, and explain how it affects the capacitance.
