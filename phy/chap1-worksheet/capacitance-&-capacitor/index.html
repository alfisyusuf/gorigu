01 · Foundations

What is a Capacitor?

A capacitor is a component that stores electric charge — and with it, electric potential energy — inside an electric field. In its simplest form it is two conducting plates placed close together, separated by an insulating material called a dielectric.

When the plates are connected to a voltage source (like a battery), electrons are pushed onto one plate and pulled off the other. One plate ends up with excess positive charge \(+Q\), the other with equal and opposite charge \(-Q\). No charge actually crosses the dielectric — the capacitor stores energy in the electric field that forms between the plates.

02 · The Core Idea

Defining Capacitance

Capacitance (\(C\)) tells you how much charge a capacitor can store for every volt of potential difference applied across it. A capacitor with a large capacitance can hold a lot of charge without needing a large voltage.

\[ C = \frac{Q}{V} \]
\(Q\) = charge stored (Coulombs, C)
\(V\) = potential difference (Volts, V)
\(C\) = capacitance (Farads, F)

Common units: \(1 \, \mu\text{F} = 10^{-6} \text{ F}\)
03 · Geometry Matters

The Parallel-Plate Capacitor

For two flat plates facing each other, capacitance depends only on their geometry and the material between them:

\[ C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d} \]
\(\varepsilon_0\) = \( 8.85 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m} \)
\(\varepsilon_r\) = dielectric constant (1 for air)
\(A\) = area of one plate (\(\text{m}^2\))
\(d\) = distance between plates (\(\text{m}\))
Area (\(A\)) ↑

Larger overlapping plate area gives more room to store charge — capacitance increases.

Distance (\(d\)) ↓

Smaller separation between plates means a stronger field for the same voltage — capacitance increases.

Dielectric (\(\varepsilon_r\)) ↑

A better insulating dielectric lets the plates hold more charge at the same voltage.

04 · In Circuits

Capacitors in Series and Parallel

Just like resistors, capacitors combine differently depending on how they're wired.

Series

\[ \frac{1}{C_{\text{total}}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \dots \]
Hint for 2 Capacitors: Avoid fractions by using the product-over-sum rule:
\[ C_{\text{total}} = \frac{C_1 \times C_2}{C_1 + C_2} \]
  • Every capacitor stores the same charge (\(Q_1 = Q_2 = Q_{\text{total}}\)).
  • The total voltage splits across each capacitor.
  • Total capacitance is always smaller than the smallest individual \(C\).

Parallel

\[ C_{\text{total}} = C_1 + C_2 + \dots \]
  • Every capacitor feels the same voltage (\(V_1 = V_2 = V_{\text{total}}\)).
  • The total charge splits across each capacitor.
  • Total capacitance is always larger than any individual \(C\).
05 · Storing Energy

Energy Stored in a Capacitor

Charging a capacitor takes work, because each new bit of charge has to be pushed against the charge already sitting on the plates. That work is stored as electric potential energy:

\[ U = \frac{1}{2}QV = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C} \]
Any of the three forms works — pick whichever variables you already know based on the question. Energy \(U\) is measured in Joules (J).
06 · Why It Matters

Where You'll Find Capacitors

Camera flash

Charges slowly from a small battery, then releases all its energy in a fraction of a second for a bright flash.

Defibrillators

Store energy over a few seconds, then deliver a large, controlled pulse to the heart much faster than a battery ever could alone.

Memory (DRAM)

Each bit of data in computer memory is stored as a tiny charge on a microscopic capacitor.

Smoothing & touchscreens

Smooth out bumpy power-supply voltages, and detect the tiny change in capacitance created by your finger on a touchscreen.
