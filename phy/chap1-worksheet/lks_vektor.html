Instructions: The positive (+) and negative (−) signs on charges are only used to determine the direction of the force arrows (Attract/Repel). Do not include the minus sign in your Coulomb's Law calculations!

1. Collinear Vectors

Determine the direction of the force first. Right = Positive, Left = Negative. The resultant is the algebraic sum.

Resultant: \( \Sigma F = F_{BC} - F_{BA} \)

2. Perpendicular Vectors

If two forces form a \( 90^\circ \) angle, use the Pythagorean theorem. Trick: Remember 3-4-5 pairs!

3. Skill Test (Workspace)

Problem 1 (Collinear Forces): Three collinear charges. Charge A (\(+2 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the left, charge B (\(-1 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is in the middle, and charge C (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the right. The distance from A to B is \(3 \, \text{cm}\), and from B to C is \(3 \, \text{cm}\). Determine the magnitude and direction of the total Coulomb force experienced by charge B!

Problem 2 (Perpendicular Fields): Point P is located \(3 \, \text{cm}\) exactly above charge Q1 (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) and \(4 \, \text{cm}\) to the left of charge Q2 (\(-3 \, \mu\text{C}\)). If the constant \( k = 9 \times 10^9 \), calculate the total electric field strength at point P!

Problem 3 (Resultant Challenge): Two attractive electrical forces act on a test charge. The first force is \( 6 \, \text{N} \) pointing North, and the second force is \( 8 \, \text{N} \) pointing East. What is the magnitude of the resultant force experienced by the charge?

Problem 4 (Midpoint Field): Point A is located exactly in the middle between charge Q1 (\(+2 \, \mu\text{C}\)) and charge Q2 (\(-4 \, \mu\text{C}\)), which are separated by a total distance of \(6 \, \text{cm}\). Calculate the total electric field strength and determine its direction at point A!

Problem 5 (Perpendicular Forces): A test charge experiences a Coulomb force of \( 15 \, \text{N} \) to the West and \( 20 \, \text{N} \) to the South due to nearby charges. Determine the magnitude of the resultant force acting on the test charge!
