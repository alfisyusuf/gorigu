Instructions: The positive (+) and negative (−) signs on charges are only used to determine the direction of the force arrows (Attract/Repel). Do not include the minus sign in your Coulomb's Law calculations!

1. Collinear Vectors

Determine the direction of the force first. Right = Positive, Left = Negative. The resultant is the algebraic sum.

Resultant: \( \Sigma F = F_{BC} - F_{BA} \)

2. Perpendicular Vectors

If two forces form a \( 90^\circ \) angle, use the Pythagorean theorem. Trick: Remember 3-4-5 pairs!

3. Skill Test (Workspace)

Problem 1 (Collinear): Three collinear charges. Charge A (\(+2 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the left, charge B (\(-1 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is in the middle, and charge C (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the right. The distance from A to B is \(3 \, \text{cm}\), and from B to C is \(3 \, \text{cm}\). Determine the magnitude and direction of the total Coulomb force experienced by charge B!

Problem 2 (Perpendicular): Point P is located \(3 \, \text{cm}\) exactly above charge Q1 (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) and \(4 \, \text{cm}\) to the left of charge Q2 (\(-3 \, \mu\text{C}\)). If the constant \( k = 9 \times 10^9 \), calculate the total electric field strength at point P!

Problem 3 (Resultant Challenge): Two attractive electrical forces act on a charge. The first force is \( 6 \, \text{N} \) pointing North, and the second force is \( 8 \, \text{N} \) pointing East. What is the magnitude of the resultant force experienced by the charge?

Problem 4 (Zero Field Concept): Charge A (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) and Charge B (\(+9 \, \mu\text{C}\)) are separated by a distance of \(10 \, \text{cm}\). Where should a point P be placed along the line between them so that the total electric field at P is exactly zero?

Hint: The electric fields from A and B must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction (\(E_A = E_B\)).

Problem 5 (2D Resultant on a Triangle): Three charges are placed at the corners of a right-angled triangle. \(Q_1\) (\(+3 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is at the top, \(Q_2\) (\(-4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is at the right angle (bottom-left), and \(Q_3\) (\(+3 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is at the bottom-right. The distance from \(Q_1\) to \(Q_2\) is \(3 \, \text{cm}\), and \(Q_2\) to \(Q_3\) is \(4 \, \text{cm}\). Determine the total Coulomb force on \(Q_2\).

Hint: Calculate the force from \(Q_1\) and \(Q_3\) on \(Q_2\). Since it's a right triangle, use Pythagoras for the final resultant.
