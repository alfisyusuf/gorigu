Instructions: The positive (+) and negative (−) signs on charges are only used to determine the direction of the force arrows (Attract/Repel). Do not include the minus sign in your Coulomb's Law calculations!

1. Collinear Vectors (1 Dimension)

If the charges are arranged in a straight line, determine the direction of the force first. Force to the right = Positive, Force to the left = Negative. The resultant is the algebraic sum.

Resultant force on B:   \( \Sigma F = F_{BC} - F_{BA} \)

2. Perpendicular Vectors (Right-Angled)

If two forces are perpendicular to each other (forming a \( 90^\circ \) angle), use the Pythagorean theorem to find the resultant.
Trick: Remember the 3-4-5 and 6-8-10 number pairs!

3. Skill Test (Workspace)

Problem 1 (Collinear): Three collinear charges. Charge A (\(+2 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the left, charge B (\(-1 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is in the middle, and charge C (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) is on the right. The distance from A to B is \(3 \, \text{cm}\), and from B to C is \(3 \, \text{cm}\). Determine the magnitude and direction of the total Coulomb force experienced by charge B!

Hint: Calculate \( F_{BA} \) and \( F_{BC} \) separately first.

Problem 2 (Perpendicular): Point P is located \(3 \, \text{cm}\) exactly above charge Q1 (\(+4 \, \mu\text{C}\)) and \(4 \, \text{cm}\) to the left of charge Q2 (\(-3 \, \mu\text{C}\)). If the constant \( k = 9 \times 10^9 \), calculate the total electric field strength at point P!

Hint: Draw the directions of \( E_1 \) and \( E_2 \) from point P first. Are they perpendicular to each other?

Problem 3 (Resultant Challenge): Two attractive electrical forces act on a charge. The first force is \( 6 \, \text{N} \) pointing North, and the second force is \( 8 \, \text{N} \) pointing East. What is the magnitude of the resultant force experienced by the charge?
