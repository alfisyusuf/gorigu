Instructions: The positive (+) and negative (−) signs on charges are only used to determine the direction of the force arrows (Attract/Repel). Do not include the minus sign in your Coulomb's Law calculations!

1. Collinear Vectors (1 Dimension)

If the charges are arranged in a straight line, determine the direction of the force first. Force to the right = Positive, Force to the left = Negative. The resultant is the algebraic sum.

Resultant force on B: ΣF = F_BC - F_BA

2. Perpendicular Vectors (Right-Angled)

If two forces are perpendicular to each other (forming a 90° angle), use the Pythagorean theorem to find the resultant.
Trick: Remember the 3-4-5 and 6-8-10 number pairs!

3. Skill Test (Workspace)

Problem 1 (Collinear): Three collinear charges. Charge A (+2 μC) is on the left, charge B (-1 μC) is in the middle, and charge C (+4 μC) is on the right. The distance from A to B is 3 cm, and from B to C is 3 cm. Determine the magnitude and direction of the total Coulomb force experienced by charge B!

Hint: Calculate F_BA and F_BC separately first.

Problem 2 (Perpendicular): Point P is located 3 cm exactly above charge Q1 (+4 μC) and 4 cm to the left of charge Q2 (-3 μC). If the constant k = 9×10⁹, calculate the total electric field strength at point P!

Hint: Draw the directions of E1 and E2 from point P first. Are they perpendicular to each other?

Problem 3 (Resultant Challenge): Two attractive electrical forces act on a charge. The first force is 6 Newtons pointing North, and the second force is 8 Newtons pointing East. What is the magnitude of the resultant force experienced by the charge?
