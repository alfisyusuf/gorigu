01 · Big idea

What is a capacitor?

A capacitor is a small device that stores electric charge — like a tiny, fast-charging bucket for electricity. Drag the sliders below and watch it fill up.

Charge stored (Q = C × V) 0µC

CCapacitance, in one line

Capacitance tells you how much charge a capacitor can hold for every volt pushed across it. A bigger capacitance means more charge stored at the same voltage.

C = QV   Unit: Farad (F). One Farad is huge, so real capacitors are usually measured in µF (microfarad), nF (nanofarad) or pF (picofarad).

02 · Structure

What's inside a capacitor?

Every capacitor is really just two conducting plates, separated by a thin insulating layer called the dielectric. Change the shape below to see what affects capacitance.

iWhat just happened?

Bigger plates → more room to store charge → capacitance goes up. Plates further apart → weaker attraction between them → capacitance goes down. A better dielectric lets the plates "feel" each other more strongly, so capacitance goes up too.

03 · Behaviour

Charging & discharging

Connect a capacitor to a battery through a resistor, and it doesn't fill up instantly — it charges smoothly along a curve. Press the button and watch the voltage rise.

τThe RC time constant

The speed of charging depends on resistance (R) and capacitance (C):

τ = R × C

τ = 0.88 s — after about 5τ, the capacitor is considered fully charged.

04 · Real world

Types & everyday uses

Capacitors come in different shapes and materials, each suited to a different job.

🔵

Ceramic

Small, cheap, no polarity. Used in nearly every circuit board.

🥫

Electrolytic

Cylinder-shaped, stores a lot of charge. Used in power supplies.

📗

Film

Stable and precise. Used in audio and timing circuits.

🟠

Tantalum

Compact and reliable, used in phones and small gadgets.

🎛️

Variable

Capacitance can be adjusted — used to tune radios.

⚡

Supercapacitor

Enormous capacitance, charges and releases energy very fast.

Running this in class

A simple 4-step flow · about 35–40 minutes total

1
Open together (5 min)

Project the Home tab. Let one student drag the Voltage and Capacitance sliders while the class watches the plates fill with charge.

2
Explore in pairs (10 min)

Students open Inside and Circuit on their own devices, changing area, distance, dielectric, R and C, and note what increases capacitance.

3
Discuss real uses (10 min)

Browse the Types tab as a class. Ask: which capacitor type would you choose for a camera flash, and why?

4
Quiz individually (10–15 min)

Students complete the Quiz tab alone, then compare scores with a neighbour before whole-class review.
