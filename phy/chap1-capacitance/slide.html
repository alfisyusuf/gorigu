04Foundations⏱ 3 min

What is a Capacitor?

A capacitor is a component that stores electric charge — and with it, electric potential energy — in an electric field.

In its simplest form, it's just two conducting plates placed close together, separated by an insulating material called the dielectric.

No charge ever crosses the gap. Instead, energy is stored in the field between the plates.

05Foundations⏱ 3 min

Why can it store charge?

When the plates are connected to a battery, electrons are pushed onto one plate and pulled off the other one — through the wires, never through the gap.

One plate ends up with excess positive charge +Q, the other with equal and opposite charge −Q. The insulating dielectric stops the charges from jumping across and cancelling out — so the charge just sits there, stored, until the circuit is closed again.

True

07Foundations⏱ 3 min

Defining Capacitance

Capacitance (C) tells you how much charge a capacitor can store for every volt applied across it.

\( C = \dfrac{Q}{V} \quad|\quad Q = C \times V \quad|\quad V = \dfrac{Q}{C} \)
\( C = \dfrac{Q}{V} \)
Q = charge stored (Coulombs, C)

11Geometry⏱ 3 min

The Parallel-Plate Capacitor

For two flat plates facing each other, capacitance depends only on their geometry and the material between them.

\( C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d} \)
\(\varepsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}\text{ F/m}\)  ·  \(\varepsilon_r\) = dielectric constant (1 for air)  ·  \(A\) = plate area (m²)  ·  \(d\) = plate separation (m)
Area (A) ↑

Bigger plates → more room for charge → C increases.

Distance (d) ↓

Closer plates → stronger field → C increases.

Dielectric (εᵣ) ↑

A better insulator lets plates hold more charge → C increases.

14Circuits⏱ 4 min

Capacitors in Series

\( \dfrac{1}{C_{\text{total}}} = \dfrac{1}{C_1} + \dfrac{1}{C_2} + \dots \)
  • Every capacitor stores the same charge: \(Q_1 = Q_2 = Q_{\text{total}}\)
  • The total voltage splits across each capacitor.
  • Total capacitance is always smaller than the smallest individual C.
15Circuits⏱ 4 min

Capacitors in Parallel

\( C_{\text{total}} = C_1 + C_2 + \dots \)
  • Every capacitor feels the same voltage: \(V_1 = V_2 = V_{\text{total}}\)
  • The total charge splits across each capacitor.
  • Total capacitance is always larger than any individual C.
16Compare⏱ 2 min

Series vs. Parallel — side by side

Series

Same charge on each capacitor. Voltage divides. Behaves like adding resistance to charging — total C shrinks.

Parallel

Same voltage on each capacitor. Charge divides. Behaves like more plate area — total C grows.

Ask the class: "why do series combinations always give a smaller total, while parallel gives a larger one?"

17Worked Example⏱ 4 min

Series circuit: work it out

\( C_1 = 6\ \mu\text{F} \), \( C_2 = 3\ \mu\text{F} \), supply \( V = 18\text{ V} \), connected in series. Find \(C_{\text{total}}\), \(Q_{\text{total}}\), and the voltage across each capacitor.

1\( C_{\text{total}} = \dfrac{C_1 C_2}{C_1+C_2} = \dfrac{6\times3}{6+3} = 2\ \mu\text{F} \)
2\( Q_{\text{total}} = C_{\text{total}} \times V = 2\times18 = 36\ \mu\text{C} \) (same on both, since series)
3\( V_1 = Q/C_1 = 36/6 = 6\text{ V} \)  ·  \( V_2 = Q/C_2 = 36/3 = 12\text{ V} \)  →  \(6+12=18\text{ V}\) ✓
18Worked Example⏱ 4 min

Parallel circuit: work it out

\( C_3 = 4\ \mu\text{F} \), \( C_4 = 2\ \mu\text{F} \), supply \( V = 10\text{ V} \), connected in parallel. Find \(C_{\text{total}}\) and the charge on each capacitor.

1\( C_{\text{total}} = C_3+C_4 = 4+2 = 6\ \mu\text{F} \)
2Both feel the same 10 V (parallel).
3\( Q_3 = C_3 V = 4\times10 = 40\ \mu\text{C} \)  ·  \( Q_4 = C_4 V = 2\times10 = 20\ \mu\text{C} \)

20Energy⏱ 4 min

Energy Stored in a Capacitor

Charging a capacitor takes work — each new bit of charge is pushed against the charge already on the plates. That work becomes stored energy.

\( U = \dfrac{1}{2}QV = \dfrac{1}{2}CV^2 = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C} \)
Pick whichever form uses the variables you already know. U is in Joules (J).

Think of it like a Q–V graph: the energy stored is the area of the triangle under the line, which is why there's a ½ in the formula.

21Worked Example⏱ 3 min

Try it: find the energy stored

A 220 µF capacitor is charged to 25 V. Calculate the energy stored.

1\( C = 220\times10^{-6}\text{ F} \), \( V=25\text{ V} \) → use \( U=\frac12 CV^2 \)
2\( U = 0.5 \times 220\times10^{-6} \times 25^2 \)
3\( U \approx 0.0688\text{ J} = 68.8\text{ mJ} \)

23Turn & Talk⏱ 3 min

Discuss with a partner

"Why does a camera flash use a capacitor, instead of powering the bulb directly from the battery?"

02:00

A small battery can't push out a burst of current fast enough to make a bright flash instantly. By slowly charging a capacitor first, all that stored energy can be released almost instantly — much faster than the battery could deliver it directly.
